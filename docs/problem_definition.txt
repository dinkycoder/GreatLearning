--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BUSINESS CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The number of restaurants in New York is growing rapidly, and a large share of
students and busy working professionals depend on them because of their hectic
lifestyles. Online food delivery is an attractive option for this group, giving
them access to food from their favorite restaurants without leaving home or work.

FoodHub is a food-aggregator company that brings multiple restaurants together
inside a single smartphone app. The end-to-end flow is:

    Customer places order in app
        -> Restaurant confirms the order
        -> FoodHub assigns a delivery person
        -> Delivery person picks up the food (confirms pick-up in app)
        -> Delivery person drops off the food (confirms drop-off in app)
        -> Customer optionally rates the order

FoodHub monetizes this flow by collecting a fixed margin (commission) on each
delivery order from the restaurants. Consequently, FoodHub's revenue scales with
order volume and order value, while its brand strength depends on a fast,
reliable, well-rated delivery experience.

FoodHub has stored the order-level data generated by its registered customers on
the online portal and wants to mine it for insight into restaurant demand,
operational performance, and customer satisfaction, so it can improve the overall
customer experience and grow the business.

Dataset in scope: data/raw/foodhub_order.csv
  - 1,898 orders placed by 1,200 unique customers (416 customers ordered more
    than once)
  - 178 restaurants across 14 cuisine types
  - Order cost ranges from $4.47 to $35.41 (mean ~$16.50)
  - ~71% of orders are placed on weekends, ~29% on weekdays
  - Food preparation time: 20-35 min (mean ~27 min)
  - Delivery time: 15-33 min (mean ~24 min)
  - Customer rating is out of 5, but ~39% of orders have no rating ("Not given")

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROJECT OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Analyze FoodHub's historical order data to build a clear, data-driven picture of
(a) which restaurants and cuisines drive demand, (b) how efficiently orders are
fulfilled, and (c) how satisfied customers are with the service. Translate these
findings into concrete, actionable recommendations that help FoodHub enhance the
customer experience, protect its delivery-margin revenue, and prioritize the
restaurants and operational levers that matter most.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS (KPIs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KPI 1 - Total Fulfillment Time (operational efficiency)
    Definition : food_preparation_time + delivery_time, measured per order (min).
    Why it matters: This is the customer's real wait from confirmation to
        doorstep. Shorter, more consistent fulfillment times drive satisfaction
        and repeat ordering. Tracked as the average total time, plus its spread,
        segmented by weekday/weekend, cuisine, and restaurant.

KPI 2 - Customer Satisfaction (experience quality)
    Definition : Average customer rating (out of 5) among rated orders, together
        with the share of rated orders that are "promoters" (rating >= 4) and the
        rating coverage (% of orders that received any rating at all).
    Why it matters: Ratings are the most direct signal of experience quality.
        The very high "Not given" rate (~39%) is itself a KPI-worthy gap, because
        low rating coverage limits FoodHub's ability to detect problems.

KPI 3 - Revenue & Demand Concentration (business value)
    Definition : Total order value and Average Order Value (AOV), plus the share
        of total orders and revenue contributed by the top restaurants and top
        cuisines. Because FoodHub earns a fixed margin per order, order value is a
        direct proxy for commission revenue.
    Why it matters: Reveals which partners and cuisines the business depends on,
        where demand is concentrated, and where growth or risk is greatest.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. KEY HYPOTHESES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hypothesis 1 - Weekend orders take longer to fulfill.
    Statement : The mean total fulfillment time (prep + delivery) is HIGHER for
        orders placed on weekends than on weekdays, because weekend demand strains
        both kitchens and the delivery fleet.
    Rationale : ~71% of orders fall on weekends; if capacity does not scale with
        demand, fulfillment time should rise.
    Testable via: comparison of mean total time for Weekend vs Weekday
        (two-sample test).

Hypothesis 2 - A better (faster) delivery experience earns higher ratings.
    Statement : Orders with SHORTER total fulfillment time receive HIGHER customer
        ratings; i.e., delivery speed and customer rating are inversely related.
    Rationale : Wait time is a primary driver of perceived service quality, so
        slower orders should correlate with lower ratings.
    Testable via: association between total fulfillment time and rating
        (correlation / group comparison across rating levels).

Hypothesis 3 - Demand and revenue are concentrated in a few restaurants/cuisines.
    Statement : A small subset of restaurants and cuisine types accounts for a
        disproportionately large share of total orders and total order value
        (a Pareto-style concentration).
    Rationale : Popularity and brand tend to concentrate demand; identifying the
        vital few partners is critical for retention and negotiation.
    Testable via: cumulative share of orders/revenue by ranked restaurant and
        cuisine (descriptive concentration analysis).

Notes / Assumptions
- "cost_of_the_order" is treated as the gross order value; FoodHub's commission
  is a fixed (unspecified) fraction of it, so order value ranks proxy revenue.
- "Not given" ratings are treated as missing (not as a low score) and are handled
  explicitly during data cleaning and satisfaction analysis.
- Analysis is descriptive/diagnostic on historical data; no causal claims are made
  beyond what the tests support.
