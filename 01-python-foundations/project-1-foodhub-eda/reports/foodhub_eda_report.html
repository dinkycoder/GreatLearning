Exploratory Data Analysis & Hypothesis Testing

1,898 orders · 178 restaurants · 14 cuisines · 1,200 customers

1Problem Statement

FoodHub is a New York food-aggregator app connecting customers to many restaurants and earning a fixed margin on every delivered order. Its revenue scales with order volume and value, while its brand depends on a fast, reliable, well-rated delivery experience.

Objective: analyze historical order data to understand which restaurants and cuisines drive demand, how efficiently orders are fulfilled, and how satisfied customers are — and turn that into actions that improve the customer experience and protect delivery-margin revenue.

Hypotheses tested

  • H1 — Weekend orders take longer to fulfill than weekday orders.
  • H2 — Faster fulfillment is associated with higher customer ratings.
  • H3 — Demand & revenue are concentrated in a few restaurants / cuisines.

2Data Overview

One row per order; nine original fields covering the restaurant, cuisine, cost, day type, rating, and the prep & delivery times. The data is clean — no duplicates and no missing values apart from unrated orders.

5Key Insights

1. The business rests on a concentrated core. The top 5 of 178 restaurants take ~33% of orders and revenue, and the top 4 cuisines cover ~83%. Revenue follows volume, not price (order cost is similar across cuisines).
2. Weekdays — not weekends — are the delivery bottleneck. Despite 2.5× the volume, weekends fulfil faster; the slower weekday delivery leg is the real efficiency target.
3. Speed is not the satisfaction lever. Ratings are flat across fast and slow deliveries, so faster delivery alone won't lift ratings.
4. Satisfaction is under-measured. ~39% of orders are unrated and scores never fall below 3 — the service looks great, but only among self-selected raters.
5. Loyalty is frequency, not basket size. Repeat customers (~35%) generate most orders but spend the same per order — growth comes from ordering more often, not bigger baskets.

6Recommendations

1
Double down on the core partners. Secure and grow the top restaurants/cuisines (loyalty terms, joint promos); they are both the biggest asset and the biggest concentration risk.
2
Attack weekday delivery time. Target the weekday delivery leg (routing, driver positioning during traffic) rather than over-investing in weekend capacity that already performs.
3
Lift rating coverage. Add a simple post-delivery rating nudge; closing the ~39% response gap is the fastest way to actually see problems before improving them.
4
Convert one-time to repeat. Focus retention on frequency — first-order follow-ups, re-order prompts for lapsed customers — since loyalty, not basket size, compounds value.
5
Grow the long tail selectively. Help promising low-volume restaurants gain visibility to reduce dependence on a handful of venues.

7Scope & Caveats

  • No order timestamps — no true time trends or retention cohorts; "loyalty" uses a repeat-vs-one-time proxy.
  • Rating non-response (~39%) and a compressed 3–5 scale — satisfaction findings describe self-selected raters.
  • No basket detail, delivery distance, or SLA target — "items per order", "delivery delay" and "on-time rate" can't be computed; revenue is proxied by order cost.

These define the analysis boundaries and double as FoodHub's data wish-list: capture order timestamps, delivery distance/SLA, basket contents, and lift the rating-response rate.
